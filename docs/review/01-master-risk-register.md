**Status:** 🔴 Gate-blocking · **Owner:** Review Board Chair · **Date:** 2026-07-14
**Inputs:** 160 raw adversarial findings from 25 specialist reviewers over the ratified NEXUS Phase-0 architecture (docs 01–10 + ADR-0001…0010).
**Scope of judgement:** NEXUS is **pure-referral** (no checkout / no custody / no card data — ADR-0006) and **phased-rollout** (ADR-0007). Findings that assumed custody or a single-shot global launch were rejected. Automated validation (links, 64 Mermaid, 10/10 ADR refs, referral consistency, cost & threat coverage) already PASSED and is factored into the documentation score only.

### 1.4 Top themes (highest consensus / systemic)

1. **Attribution & revenue integrity is the platform's soft underbelly** — 12 issues; the sole source of truth (async, reversible postbacks) has no leak-detection, no cross-network purchase-correlation key, and failover semantics that can double-pay or orphan referrals.
2. **The AI cost ceiling (≤$0.01) structurally collides with the trust guarantees (grounding, hallucination, price accuracy)** — 16 issues; cost governance can silently degrade exactly the money-adjacent reasoning and verification the platform's trust rests on.
3. **"Multi-region / multi-cloud / residency" claims outrun the provisioned topology** — 9 issues; write-home money/PII is a single-region SPOF, DR pairs are unspecified for regulated zones, and infra lands after the markets that legally require it.
4. **Single-writer/single-instance concentration masquerading as "no SPOF"** — the Ledger (shared kernel), the Affiliate Gateway (shared router), the egress proxy, and the AI routing-policy config are each a systemic blast radius.
5. **Fitness-function theatre** — several ratified CI gates (neutrality golden test, rollback-doc present, connector conformance/failover, AI eval) enforce *shape/presence*, not the *property* they claim to guarantee.

## 3. Critical & High detail

> Format per item: **Root cause · Impact · Probability · Recommended solution · Alternatives · Trade-offs · Effort · Affected docs · ADR needed.**

### R-001 — Attribution under-reporting is undetectable; no settlement reconciliation *(Critical · revenue-integrity · raisedBy 5)*
- **Root cause:** ADR-0006 makes 100% of primary revenue depend on async, network-controlled postbacks; NEXUS never observes the sale (04 §5.4). 08 §8.2 reconciles the ledger against *partner settlement reports* (same party that under-reports), so silent under-reporting/missing postbacks/attribution loss have no independent detector.
- **Impact:** Continuous, invisible revenue leak; VMS north-star and CFO >80%-margin guardrail computed on a systematically understated numerator with no alarm.
- **Probability:** High.
- **Recommended solution:** Build an attribution-reconciliation subsystem that (a) compares NEXUS's own `handoff.redirected` click-out log against each network's click/conversion reporting API and a sampled panel of real test purchases to compute a per-network/region `attribution_gap_rate`; (b) baselines expected conversion rates per merchant/category and pages on anomalous drop-off; feed both into the existing FinOps/anomaly infra (ADR-0009) as first-class revenue SLIs.
- **Alternatives:** Direct merchant server-to-server webhooks for top merchants (attribution certainty, integration cost); accept the leak as a modeled fraud/margin budget.
- **Trade-offs:** Reconciliation depends on networks exposing reporting APIs (not guaranteed for all 4) and adds ops surface; but the alternative is an unmonitored existential revenue leak.
- **Effort:** L · **Docs:** 03, 04, 06, ADR-0006 · **ADR needed:** Yes (attribution-reliability decision).

### R-002 — Cross-network duplicate-conversion / failover double-attribution; no purchase-correlation key *(Critical · revenue-integrity · raisedBy 6)*
- **Root cause:** ADR-0008 allows an offer to be sourced via multiple connectors and mandates failover; 06 §3.7/§3.8 key conversions only per-network (`network_txn_id`), with no merchant-order/global purchase key. 08 §5.6 *mandates* cross-provider dedup but no doc specifies a mechanism that works without an order key (impossible under pure-referral). Mid-session failover can also re-stamp attribution on a second connector.
- **Impact:** One physical purchase generates two valid postbacks from two networks → double cashback/commission paid; or aggressive dedup denies legitimate double-purchases. Recurring, silent, scales with fraud-ring sophistication.
- **Probability:** High.
- **Recommended solution:** Introduce a probabilistic `purchase_fingerprint` (merchant + normalized offer + user + amount bucket + time bucket) computed at postback-ingest; a second confirmed conversion matching a fingerprint within the longest cookie window routes to a hold/adjudication queue, not auto-credit. Pin one connector per offer+session for the attribution window; degrade to cached data rather than mid-session connector swap. Expose a "suspected duplicate conversions" FinOps metric from day one.
- **Alternatives:** One connector per merchant/user session (no same-offer failover); negotiate order-hash from networks that expose it and trust-weight those that don't.
- **Trade-offs:** Fingerprinting adds latency and false-positive holds at the moment revenue is recognized; sticky-connector reduces the failover resilience ADR-0008 exists to provide. Document dedup as probabilistic and size a fraud-loss budget.
- **Effort:** L · **Docs:** 04, 06, 08, ADR-0006, ADR-0008 · **ADR needed:** Yes.

### R-003 — Forged-postback ledger drain; "signature-verified per provider" overstated *(Critical · security-risk · raisedBy 2)*
- **Root cause:** 08 §5.6/§7.1 assert postbacks are "signature-verified per provider" as a NEXUS-owned control; real affiliate postbacks are frequently plain GETs keyed on txn/click id with weak or network-controlled auth (IP allow-list/shared secret), not an HMAC NEXUS validates end-to-end.
- **Impact:** An attacker who learns one network's postback format fires forged conversions at the Gateway ingest; cashback/commission accrue as real payable liabilities for sales that never happened.
- **Probability:** High.
- **Recommended solution:** Make postback-derived accrual **provisional by construction** — held non-payable until independently reconciled against the network's settlement/reporting feed (pull, not push). Replace the "signature-verified" MUST with a concrete per-connector auth table (actual scheme/algorithm/secret-rotation per network), treating "signed" as unverified until validated per connector. (08 §5.6 already says disputed attribution is "held, not paid" — extend that to *all* postback accrual, not just disputed.)
- **Alternatives:** Postback-only accrual with tighter velocity/anomaly caps + fast clawback (leaves a payout-before-verification window).
- **Trade-offs:** Delays visible cashback (worse UX) and requires settlement-feed ingestion per network; closes an existential fraud vector rather than detecting it post-hoc.
- **Effort:** L · **Docs:** 07, 08, ADR-0008 · **ADR needed:** Yes (accrual-finality/reconciliation policy).

### R-004 — Ledger is a shared-kernel SPOF across four money domains *(Critical · architectural-weakness · raisedBy 1)*
- **Root cause:** 04/06 route Affiliate accrual, Cashback, Rewards, and Creator payouts through one double-entry "Ledger & Payouts" while calling the design "bounded contexts, no cross-context DB access." A single global double-entry ledger cannot be an independently-consistent bounded context without a shared consistency domain or an undocumented distributed-saga; the "extraction is mechanical" claim is false for this module.
- **Impact:** Every money-touching domain has a hard consistency dependency on one component; a creator-payout schema change can corrupt cashback invariants; the Ledger cannot be extracted without a distributed-transaction protocol across four domains.
- **Probability:** High.
- **Recommended solution:** Ledger-per-context: each domain owns an append-only accrual log; a dedicated Reconciliation/GL service aggregates them asynchronously into the audited double-entry view, scoping the invariant per sub-ledger. Explicitly declare the GL as a **named shared-kernel exception** with an ADR if kept unified.
- **Alternatives:** Keep one Ledger but as its own bounded context with a hard synchronous API only + consumer-driven contract tests (still single-writer contention).
- **Trade-offs:** Per-domain ledgers add reconciliation latency/eventual-consistency in financial reporting; a single ledger is simpler to audit but is the coupling risk itself.
- **Effort:** L · **Docs:** 02, 04, 06 · **ADR needed:** Yes.

### R-005 — No hold-window gate between cashback accrual and wallet payout *(Critical · architectural-weakness · raisedBy 3)*
- **Root cause:** 09 §9 gives cashback a hold/clawback window, but WALLET (06 §3.5) exposes a single `balance_cash` with no split between available and held, and no rule blocks payout until `CASHBACK_ACCRUAL.state = confirmed`. Compounds with the stored-value liability question (R-037).
- **Impact:** A user can request payout during the hold window; real money leaves via the payout partner; a later network reversal posts a reversing ledger entry but the cash is gone and NEXUS has no custody to claw back — unrecoverable leak + a direct fraud recipe (accrue, pay out, return goods).
- **Probability:** High.
- **Recommended solution:** Model wallet as two ledger accounts per user (`cash_available`, `cash_held`); the payout API reads only `cash_available`; held cashback migrates to available only after the hold window (aligned to the merchant's actual return window, not just postback latency), enforced by DB constraint / serializable check.
- **Alternatives:** Mandatory minimum hold (30–90 d) before eligibility (hurts payout-speed UX); partner-side payout reversal/insurance (adds cost/dependency).
- **Trade-offs:** True return-window hold conflicts with the "fast cashback" promise — a UX-vs-risk decision the docs never surface.
- **Effort:** M · **Docs:** 06 §3.5/§9 · **ADR needed:** No (schema/design fix) — but log the hold-window policy decision.

### R-006 — AI cost ceiling not carved out for grounding/claim-verification; soft-degrade can cut the trust guardrail *(Critical · cost-explosion · raisedBy 4)*
- **Root cause:** ADR-0009/05 §9.4 apply per-user/session/feature soft-degrade uniformly, with no explicit exemption for the Claim-Verification Agent, `price.check_live`, or money-adjacent (handoff) reasoning. Claim-verify runs on nearly every response and is a prime throttling candidate; nothing walls it off from cost-optimizer demotion.
- **Impact:** Under a flash-sale cost spike (peak stakes), the cost governor can silently route handoff reasoning/verification to a cheaper model or skip re-grounding — reintroducing exactly the stale/hallucinated price risk NFR-AI-01/COMP-01 exist to prevent, with no CI fitness function to catch it.
- **Probability:** High.
- **Recommended solution:** Add a normative rule + CI fitness function: Claim-Verification, live price-check at buy-intent, and citation rendering are **never** subject to soft-degrade/budget throttling; a cost breach degrades drafting/planning model tier only. Add a "stakes tier" dimension to the budget tracker with a protected floor for money-adjacent requests; compute the $0.01 blended target *excluding* an uncapped verification line.
- **Alternatives:** Hard-fail (refuse "try again") money-adjacent requests rather than silently degrade (contradicts "never hard-fail" but safer here); statistical post-hoc audit only (weaker).
- **Trade-offs:** Ring-fencing verification raises the true blended cost above the headline $0.01 and forces the squeeze onto low-stakes traffic — an honest trade the docs must acknowledge.
- **Effort:** M · **Docs:** 05 §4.3/§9.4, ADR-0009 · **ADR needed:** Yes (amend ADR-0009).

### R-007 — Cheapest-capable routing predicts quality before generation; confidence ≠ correctness *(Critical · ai-hallucination · raisedBy 1)*
- **Root cause:** 05 §2.4 selects the cheapest model *predicted* to meet a quality target before it runs, then escalates only on the model's *own* confidence `< θ` — a signal documented to be poorly correlated with factual correctness. The escalation trigger fails on exactly the failure mode (confidently wrong) it exists to catch.
- **Impact:** An adversarially-phrased buy-intent query resembling an "easy" class is routed to Tier-1, answered fluently but wrong, stays above θ, and the cost-tuned Claim-Verify under-scrutinizes it — silent hallucination leakage on the ~70% cheap-tier traffic the cascade is proudest of.
- **Probability:** High.
- **Recommended solution:** Decouple the escalation signal from the answering model's self-confidence: use an independent calibration/verification model (or ensemble-disagreement) as the escalation trigger; mandate escalation on *classifier uncertainty*, not just generation-time confidence; add a red-team metric specifically for "confidently-wrong Tier-1 answers"; route a fixed random sample of easy-classified traffic to Tier-2 as a canary to measure true classifier miss-rate.
- **Alternatives:** Always second-model cross-check on Tier-1 before Claim-Verify (cost).
- **Trade-offs:** Adds cost/latency to the cheap tier, eroding the savings — the cost/quality tension the current design resolves by assumption.
- **Effort:** L · **Docs:** 05 §2.3/§2.4/§8.5, ADR-0009 · **ADR needed:** No (mechanism) — but document the calibration decision.

### R-008 — Mandatory live-price re-check on every cache hit → affiliate rate-limit thundering herd *(Critical · scalability-bottleneck · raisedBy 3)*
- **Root cause:** The "iron rule" (05 §2.5/§9.3) re-verifies price live on price-bearing responses; price is present in nearly every shopping query. There is no coalescing of concurrent live-price lookups for the same offer, and Amazon PA-API throttles at a low, sales-tied TPS.
- **Impact:** A viral SKU makes every concurrent user a cache-HIT that still fires an individual `price.check_live`; a burst exceeds the network rate limit exactly at peak revenue, forcing mass "couldn't confirm price" degrade — the worst moment for a comparison product.
- **Probability:** High.
- **Recommended solution:** Add an in-flight request-coalescing (single-flight) layer at the `price.check_live` boundary keyed by `offer_id` (the `live:offer:*` key already exists) so N concurrent buy-intents for one offer share ONE upstream call and one freshness timestamp; add a per-offer rate governor + predictive pre-warm for rising-velocity offers. Document the sub-second coalescing window as an explicit, bounded exception to the iron rule.
- **Alternatives:** Pre-warm/poll high-traffic offers proactively.
- **Trade-offs:** Introduces a small (sub-second) freshness window at the exact point the docs currently forbid any — needs an explicit, disclosed exception.
- **Effort:** M · **Docs:** 05 §2.5/§9.1/§9.3, 06 §7, ADR-0008 · **ADR needed:** Yes (freshness-exception at high concurrency).

### R-009 — Single shared AI routing-policy config = platform-wide AI blast radius *(Critical · spof · raisedBy 1)*
- **Root cause:** 05 §1 mandates each capability be independently deployable ("a regression in Review Synthesis MUST NOT redeploy the agent"), but §2.1/§2.3/§10 put routing policy for all C1–C9 in one hot-reloadable config consumed by one Gateway, and rollback = a config revert on that shared artifact. The §8.5 auto-optimizer pushes deltas continuously.
- **Impact:** One malformed/canary-narrow policy delta degrades routing for all nine AI capabilities at once (search rerank, RAG, reco, translation, fraud, the agent) — the very "instant config-revert rollback" sold as the safety mechanism becomes the single lever for a platform-wide AI outage.
- **Probability:** Medium.
- **Recommended solution:** Partition routing policy per-capability (per bounded context) into independently versioned, independently gated, independently revertible namespaces; require canary eval to cover cross-capability regression, not just the targeted request-class; add a config schema/invariant validator before promotion.
- **Alternatives:** Keep one policy but run the full 9-capability eval suite + schema validator as a hard promotion gate.
- **Trade-offs:** Per-capability partitioning adds rollout pipelines and slightly slows config-only agility — the agility that creates the blast radius.
- **Effort:** M · **Docs:** 05 §1/§2.1/§10 · **ADR needed:** No (design refinement).

### R-010 — AI gateway has no residency fence; PII routed to out-of-region LLM *(Critical · privacy-risk · raisedBy 2)*
- **Root cause:** ADR-0005/0009 route cheapest-capable globally; 06 §11 / 08 §9.3 enforce residency for storage but the AI provider-selection decision is never wired into the `subject.region == data.region` check. 08 §6.3 only "minimizes/scrubs" prompts.
- **Impact:** A P3 EU or P4 BD/IN user's conversational query (behavioral + quasi-identifying context) is shipped to whichever LLM endpoint is cheapest — a cross-border transfer with no lawful basis, exactly what §9.3 says MUST be denied by default.
- **Probability:** High.
- **Recommended solution:** Add `residency_region` as a mandatory ABAC input to the AI router; maintain a per-region approved-provider allow-list in each jurisdiction's policy pack; filter candidate providers by residency *before* cost cascade; **fail closed** to a same-region/OSS model (or refuse) when no compliant provider exists.
- **Alternatives:** Restrict launch markets to in-region-hosted providers (raises cost); consent-only (insufficient under GDPR transfer rules).
- **Trade-offs:** Region-pinning directly conflicts with the ≤$0.01 optimization and model-agnostic flexibility in the strictest markets — a genuine goal-vs-compliance collision to resolve explicitly.
- **Effort:** L · **Docs:** 05, 06 §11, 08 §9.3, ADR-0005, ADR-0009, ADR-0007 · **ADR needed:** Yes.

### R-011 — AI eval gate blind to silent third-party model drift *(Critical · ai-hallucination · raisedBy 1)*
- **Root cause:** 10 §4 / 05 §8.4 fire the eval+red-team gate only on a NEXUS-initiated model/prompt/router change. Providers routinely update weights behind a stable API id with no NEXUS-side "change" event.
- **Impact:** Hallucination can silently breach NFR-AI-01 (<0.5%) or price accuracy NFR-COMP-01 (≥99%) the day a provider updates its model, with no trigger — a live compliance/trust failure the whole gating architecture is structurally blind to, on a path that ends in an irreversible handoff.
- **Probability:** High.
- **Recommended solution:** Treat "provider silently updated the model" as a first-class change type: run a scheduled (e.g., daily) production sample-and-score groundedness/price job per live model/provider version (the §8.3 machinery, applied continuously), with the same hard-block + automatic rollback-to-prior-pin as pre-promotion.
- **Alternatives:** Pin to immutable provider snapshots where offered (forfeits auto-improvement; not all providers support it).
- **Trade-offs:** Continuous re-eval consumes the tight ≤$0.01 budget it protects; snapshot-pinning limits model choice.
- **Effort:** M · **Docs:** 05 §8, 10 §4/§4.1 · **ADR needed:** No.

### R-012 — "Multi-cloud-capable" false; AWS-proprietary control plane unabstracted *(Critical · vendor-lock-in · raisedBy 2)*
- **Root cause:** ADR-0010/0003 promise "every subsystem replaceable, every provider adapter-wrapped," but 09 hard-wires IRSA (AWS pod identity), Global Accelerator (anycast), Route 53 geo/latency, Shield Advanced, and Control Tower/SCPs into the traffic-routing, workload-identity, and guardrail plane — none behind the adapter interfaces (which only cover KMS/Secrets/SQS/S3). This shared global edge is also the single control plane for all regions with no portability caveat.
- **Impact:** The components most needed for a second cloud or exit (global steering, workload identity, org guardrails) are exactly the ones not abstracted; "add a region on GCP/Azure without re-architecting" is false. As a single global config, a bad Route53/GA edit or IAM compromise mis-routes/blackholes US+EU+APAC at once, defeating per-region isolation.
- **Probability:** High.
- **Recommended solution:** Extend the adapter-boundary CI lint to workload-identity (SPIFFE/SPIRE) and global-routing ("traffic-steering" port with GA+Route53 as one impl, a documented second impl e.g. Cloudflare LB); add Route53/GA/Shield to the §5 portability table as **explicit irreducible lock-in**; treat global-routing changes as the highest-blast-radius change class (canary + second-approver).
- **Alternatives:** Honestly downgrade ADR-0010/0003 language to "AWS-primary with a documented, costed re-platforming plan."
- **Trade-offs:** True portability (SPIFFE, agnostic LB, dual DNS) is ongoing engineering cost and forgoes AWS-native convenience; the current path is cheaper but the "no lock-in" claim must not be sold as fact.
- **Effort:** L · **Docs:** 09 §1/§4/§5/§6, ADR-0003, ADR-0010 · **ADR needed:** Yes (amend ADR-0003/0010 lock-in claims).

### R-013 — Write-home money/PII is a single-region SPOF; no in-zone DR pair; Kafka RPO≈0 region-false *(Critical · spof · raisedBy 4)*
- **Root cause:** 06 §11 mandates write-home (PII/ledger only in home region); 10 §5/09 §6 provision exactly one region per compliance zone (eu-west-1, ap-south-1). DR targets (RTO≤30 m, RPO≤5 m; ledger RPO≈0) are written for a full-region outage, but there is no compliant in-zone failover target, and Kafka replication is intra-region (RF≥3 multi-AZ) with no documented cross-region MirrorMaker/Replicator for money topics.
- **Impact:** A genuine region outage leaves EU/AP users' wallet/ledger/identity writes with no compliant failover; the DR drill can only exercise reads/catalog; NFR-AVAIL SLOs and the RTO/RPO table are unachievable for the money path in non-US zones exactly when it matters.
- **Probability:** Medium.
- **Recommended solution:** Provision ≥2 physically-distinct regions within each compliance zone that PII may legally reside in (e.g., eu-central-1 alongside eu-west-1); confine near-synchronous Postgres/Kafka money replication to that zone; add cross-region Kafka replication for ledger/attribution topics; re-verify RTO/RPO in a chaos game-day that actually kills a full region. Tier the DR guarantee explicitly (AZ-loss vs region-loss).
- **Alternatives:** Explicitly document a compliant read-only/browse-only degrade window for a home-region outage and walk back the 99.9–99.95% SLA for single-region zones.
- **Trade-offs:** A second in-zone region ~doubles infra cost per zone at launch, conflicting with the capital-light phased philosophy; accepting degraded availability is cheap but changes the SLA commitments.
- **Effort:** XL · **Docs:** 06 §11/§13, 09 §6, 10 §8 · **ADR needed:** Yes.

### R-014 — Multi-region infra (P6) lands after EU/BD go-live (P3/P4); staging single-region *(Critical · architectural-weakness · raisedBy 2)*
- **Root cause:** 10 §12 opens market Phase 2 (CA/UK/AU) at eng-P3 and Phase 3 (EU) at eng-P4, gated only on "legal sign-off," while "multi-region rollout" and DR are P6/H3 deliverables. 06 §11 claims residency tiers open with each phase — contradicting 10's phase table. Staging is single-region (09 §12), so cross-region residency filters, write-home routing, and GA/Route53 failover are first exercised in a live regulated market.
- **Impact:** EU/UK PII/ledger has no compliant home tier for ~two phases (write EU PII into US = GDPR breach, or the "EU flag live" gate is unmeetable and dates are fiction); residency-filter bugs (R-CLD-04) surface first in production against real regulated data.
- **Probability:** High.
- **Recommended solution:** Split "multi-region" into (1) a minimal per-region residency tier (region-pinned Postgres + Kafka, single-AZ ok) pulled forward to *precede* each market phase and made a named precondition inside the §4.2 compliance gate, and (2) full DR/failover at P6. Stand up a genuine two-region staging pair before Phase 2 and make the cross-region residency-filter test a hard release gate.
- **Alternatives:** Delay EU/UK/BD market phases to P6 to synchronize infra and market (abandons the ADR-0007 timeline).
- **Trade-offs:** Building residency tiers early duplicates work superseded by P6 and roughly doubles staging cost; shipping regulated users on non-compliant infra is a far larger exposure.
- **Effort:** L · **Docs:** 06, 09, 10, ADR-0007 · **ADR needed:** Yes (phase-gate/infra-sequencing correction).

### R-015 — Erasure never reaches affiliate networks + post-erasure postback processing *(Critical · privacy-risk · raisedBy 2)*
- **Root cause:** 08 §2.3/§7.1 push click_id/sub_id/offer_id to networks at handoff; §12.4 crypto-shred erasure is scoped only to NEXUS's own stores. No mechanism notifies networks (GDPR Art. 19) or suppresses postbacks referencing an erased referral_id that arrive "minutes to days" later. (Related: the immutable event-sourced ledger/attribution trail needs per-user crypto-shred keys designed in at P1 to reconcile append-only events with Art. 17.)
- **Impact:** An erased data subject's click/conversion trail persists on four external platforms indefinitely, and NEXUS keeps *processing* postbacks tied to the erased identity for days after "erasure completed" — active post-erasure processing, not just stale third-party copies.
- **Probability:** High.
- **Recommended solution:** Model erasure as a saga: crypto-shred locally, fire a per-connector erasure/suppress notification (require this in the ADR-0008 connector interface + partner DPAs), and quarantine any postback referencing a pseudonymized/erased referral_id into a compliance-hold queue instead of auto-folding to the ledger. Adopt per-user crypto-shred keys for PII fields in event streams from P1 design time.
- **Alternatives:** Disclose the gap as a policy limitation; refuse erasure while a referral is in-flight, shred after settlement (narrows but doesn't close).
- **Trade-offs:** A cross-connector erasure protocol is per-partner integration + possible contract renegotiation; crypto-shredding adds key-management/replay complexity.
- **Effort:** L · **Docs:** 04, 06, 08 §9.2/§12.4, ADR-0008, ADR-0007 · **ADR needed:** Yes.

### R-016 — Signed-handoff redirect defeated by affiliate open-redirect (confused deputy) *(Critical · security-risk · raisedBy 1)*
- **Root cause:** Handoff legitimacy rests on allow-listing the network host + NEXUS signing the URL (08 §2.3/§5.6, 07 §4.3). But CJ/Impact/Rakuten operate as redirect proxies whose terminal destination is a network-resolved parameter; allow-listing "cj.com" constrains nothing about where the user lands.
- **Impact:** NEXUS's `signed: true` becomes a trust amplifier for a destination it never vetted; a compromised network account / rogue sub-affiliate / mis-normalized deep-link lands the user on an attacker look-alike checkout carrying NEXUS's implicit endorsement — the exact confused-deputy the signing was meant to prevent.
- **Probability:** Medium.
- **Recommended solution:** Add a merchant-domain resolution step in Handoff: follow the connector-built deep-link (no-redirect HEAD probe via the SSRF-safe egress) to the terminal host, verify against a NEXUS-owned per-merchant domain registry, and only then mint the signed session; reject/quarantine on mismatch.
- **Alternatives:** Downgrade the UX claim ("you are leaving NEXUS to Network X", no destination-vetting implication); require merchants to register destination domains directly.
- **Trade-offs:** Adds latency + a live-resolution dependency pre-signing; some networks contractually obfuscate final URLs, possibly forcing a TOS conversation.
- **Effort:** L · **Docs:** 04 §6.1, 07 §4.3/§9.1, 08 §2.3/§5.6 · **ADR needed:** No.

### R-017 — Connector plugin supply-chain: credentialed in-process layer *(Critical · vendor-lock-in/security · raisedBy 1)*
- **Root cause:** ADR-0010's "no SDK in core" pushes provider SDKs into connectors, but connectors remain first-party wrappers around third-party libs, hot-swappable, holding live vault credentials, deep-link signing input, and postback ingest — in the same Gateway runtime.
- **Impact:** A compromised upstream dependency executes exactly where trust flips to "component with a live credentialed session" — it can poison the catalog platform-wide, forge deep-links, or inject postbacks, a far larger blast radius than "core."
- **Probability:** Medium.
- **Recommended solution:** Isolate each connector in a micro-sandbox (gVisor/Firecracker or a separate least-privilege service) with per-connector IAM role + egress network policy scoped to only that provider's host; SBOM-diff + provenance attestation before enabling any connector version; pinned/vendored reproducible builds.
- **Alternatives:** In-process plugins + mandatory signature verification + runtime egress/behavior anomaly detection per connector.
- **Trade-offs:** Sandboxing adds ops complexity and latency to the HA/failover path ADR-0008 optimizes, and infra cost per connector.
- **Effort:** XL · **Docs:** 04 §6.1, 08 §5.3/§5.6/§11, ADR-0008, ADR-0010 · **ADR needed:** Yes (connector isolation model).

### R-018 — GitOps self-heal silently reverts break-glass fixes mid-incident *(Critical · architectural-weakness · raisedBy 1)*
- **Root cause:** 10 §3 makes ArgoCD the sole reconciler (auto-heal dev/staging; prod flagged OutOfSync); §11 allows MFA-gated break-glass kubectl. Any out-of-band patch is drift; nothing pauses auto-sync during an incident or forces a matching commit before the next sync tick.
- **Impact:** An SRE's sanctioned emergency patch is reverted by the next reconcile (or races the prod sync interval); the incident re-triggers minutes in and on-call burns the RTO≤30 m budget fighting the tool.
- **Probability:** High.
- **Recommended solution:** Formal "GitOps incident mode": break-glass access auto-annotates the affected Application with sync disabled (or pauses the ApplicationSet), pages a bot to land the compensating config-repo PR, and auto-re-enables sync with a forced diff review after a bounded window.
- **Alternatives:** Forbid kubectl break-glass entirely (expedited GitOps-PR only); run manual-sync everywhere (slower routine rollout).
- **Trade-offs:** Trades a "silent revert" failure for a "stuck-paused-forever" failure unless the re-enable timer/paging is itself monitored.
- **Effort:** M · **Docs:** 10 §3/§11 · **ADR needed:** No.

### R-019 — Zero EKS/Kubernetes upgrade strategy *(Critical · operational-complexity · raisedBy 1)*
- **Root cause:** 09–10 specify EKS in depth but never a control-plane version policy, node AMI rotation, API-deprecation scanning, or max version-skew. EKS forces a minor upgrade ~every 14 months — mandatory, recurring.
- **Impact:** When AWS deprecates the running minor across 3–5 prod regions, there is no runbook: no PDB-verify-before-drain, no canary-one-region-first, no automated scan for removed CRDs/APIs (Istio, Argo Rollouts, Kyverno) — a blind upgrade can break mesh + rollout + admission across a region at once.
- **Probability:** High.
- **Recommended solution:** Add a "Cluster lifecycle management" section: staged per-region EKS/AMI upgrade runbook, pre-upgrade API-deprecation scanner (kubent/pluto) as a blocking CI/scheduled gate, a mesh/CRD compatibility matrix pinned per K8s minor, a max-skew rule, and a "K8s control plane/node pool" row in the §4.1 rollback table gated like schema/infra changes.
- **Alternatives:** EKS auto-mode / extended support (delays, doesn't remove the missing runbook).
- **Trade-offs:** Formal runbook is recurring engineering cost vs. an ad-hoc deadline-pressured multi-region incident.
- **Effort:** M · **Docs:** 09 §3/§8, 10 §5/§8/§11 · **ADR needed:** No.

### R-020 — Egress allowlist single chokepoint: throughput ceiling + monetization-outage blast radius *(Critical · scalability-bottleneck · raisedBy 3)*
- **Root cause:** 09 §4 routes ALL outbound (affiliate/merchant/travel/LLM + ingestion + the revenue-bearing handoff redirect) through one domain-allowlist proxy. A stateful TLS/SNI-inspecting proxy scales with connection count, not node count; allowlist edits are "change control," not staged/canary like code.
- **Impact:** (1) At 5,000 QPS + agent fan-out + ingestion bursts, per-flow inspection CPU/connection limits become the true system ceiling, not EKS/OpenSearch. (2) A typo'd/removed allowlist entry silently blocks the deep-link redirect platform-wide — and since checkout "degrades to deep-link referral," this removes the last line of defense simultaneously with the primary path.
- **Probability:** High.
- **Recommended solution:** Tier the egress path: a stateless SNI-allowlist fast path for the small enumerable high-QPS partner/LLM domain set, deep inspection only for low-volume/new domains; version the allowlist as code with an automated conformance test (every active connector's declared domains resolve/pass) and region-by-region rollout with auto-rollback on a handoff-success-rate SLO burn; load-test the *proxy technology* at 3–4× peak connections before GA; consider per-workload egress paths.
- **Alternatives:** Per-connector allowlist scoping; fail-open-to-cache-with-badge on miss (needs Security sign-off).
- **Trade-offs:** Tiering adds a second code path to secure; per-workload egress multiplies chokepoints to harden.
- **Effort:** L · **Docs:** 09 §4, 08 §5.4, 04 · **ADR needed:** No.

### R-021 — VMS/MAU north-star metric is unfalsifiable and gameable *(Critical · hidden-assumption · raisedBy 1)*
- **Root cause:** VMS = "dollars kept vs the price they *would have* paid at their default merchant" (01 §4) — a counterfactual NEXUS constructs and self-audits, with no external ground truth, asserted as "cannot be gamed."
- **Impact:** Growth/Product have a structural incentive to inflate the counterfactual baseline (choose a higher "default", steer to high-list-price merchants) — reproducing internally, under a "verified savings" label, the fake-"was"-price dark pattern the Vision names as the enemy. It poisons the single number the whole GTM/trust loop is built on.
- **Probability:** High.
- **Recommended solution:** Publish (pre-launch) a versioned VMS spec: comparison set = median of N competing merchant prices captured at query time (not a NEXUS-chosen "default"); the selection logic is itself under the neutrality audit; log every VMS claim with its raw comparison set for post-hoc audit by a party independent of the metric-owning team.
- **Alternatives:** Demote VMS to a secondary/marketing metric; make realized cashback/coupon dollars (external ledger trail) the primary north-star.
- **Trade-offs:** A verifiable VMS is less flattering and slower-growing, weakening the viral-screenshot GTM motion.
- **Effort:** M · **Docs:** 01, 03 · **ADR needed:** Yes (metric-integrity decision).

### R-022 — No UX for promised-vs-confirmed savings (pending/clawback) *(Critical · hidden-assumption · raisedBy 1)*
- **Root cause:** The core loop asserts a specific dollar saving and shows a "tracked savings receipt" at handoff, but the receipt is only validated by an async, reversible postback (A5). No UI state machine distinguishes estimated / pending / confirmed / clawed-back.
- **Impact:** A user sees "$40 saved" instantly, then the coupon fails at the merchant cart or cashback is rejected weeks later (or never corrected) — the most trust-destroying failure a savings-proof product can have, poisoning the north-star it feeds.
- **Probability:** High.
- **Recommended solution:** Add a `savings_state` enum (estimated|pending|confirmed|reversed) to every money-bearing response (agent chat, wallet, VMS dashboard); render state-appropriate copy; surface every estimated→reversed transition as a user-visible notification, not a silent ledger entry.
- **Alternatives:** Only ever show confirmed savings (kills instant-gratification core loop).
- **Trade-offs:** Three states add UI complexity across chat/dashboard/notifications; the alternative is a metric and trust promise that quietly lies.
- **Effort:** M · **Docs:** 01, 02, 07 · **ADR needed:** No.

### R-023 — FTC disclosure architected only for sponsored, not the affiliate-commission majority *(Critical · legal-risk · raisedBy 1)*
- **Root cause:** 03 builds a neutrality wall separating labeled sponsored slots from "neutral" organic results; 08 §9.1 maps FTC obligations only to sponsored labeling. But under ADR-0006 NEXUS earns commission on essentially every driven purchase, including "neutral" recommendations; FTC Endorsement Guides require clear disclosure of any material connection at the point of recommendation.
- **Impact:** Every affiliate-linked agent recommendation is a materially-connected endorsement; disclosing only on the sponsored subset = an undisclosed-affiliate-relationship operation at platform scale from P1 — the single most enforcement-prone pattern in affiliate marketing.
- **Probability:** High.
- **Recommended solution:** A universal render-layer disclosure that fires on ANY affiliate-monetized recommendation (organic or sponsored), including in agent chat responses, with a persistent "how we make money" link; make it a launch-gate fitness function alongside the neutrality audit; legal review of the exact wording before P1.
- **Alternatives:** Restrict organic results to non-monetized comparison data (collapses primary revenue); narrow legal opinion tailoring disclosure (risk if interpretation is wrong).
- **Trade-offs:** Blanket disclosure adds friction and dilutes the "neutral AI" brand; under FTC history, over-disclose.
- **Effort:** M · **Docs:** 01, 03, 08 §9.1, ADR-0006 · **ADR needed:** No (compliance control).

### R-024 — Travel bundling = EU/UK Package-Travel/ATOL organizer liability vs no-custody *(Critical · legal-risk · raisedBy 1)*
- **Root cause:** 01 lists flight+hotel "bundling"; ADR-0006/08 assume travel liability stays on the merchant. EU Package Travel Directive 2015/2302 and UK ATOL/PTR classify facilitating combined flight+hotel bookings in one interface as a package/Linked Travel Arrangement, triggering insolvency-bonding, organizer joint liability, and pre-contractual duties — none of which exist in a no-custody referral model.
- **Impact:** Launching bundling in EU/UK (P2/P3) without a Package-Travel analysis risks NEXUS being deemed a package organizer — liable for a merchant's insolvency and subject to bonding alien to a never-holds-funds company.
- **Probability:** Medium.
- **Recommended solution:** Gate "bundling" behind its own per-jurisdiction policy-pack flag distinct from the general commerce flag, defaulting to "sequential deep-link only" (no combined package) in every EU/UK/LTA jurisdiction until a dedicated Travel-Law ADR resolves organizer status; obtain a PTD/ATOL legal opinion before enabling.
- **Alternatives:** Drop flight+hotel bundling (preserves posture, weakens the travel pitch); set up a bonded organizer entity (expensive; reopens custody exposure).
- **Trade-offs:** Restricting bundling undercuts an H2 pillar and the unified-travel moat claim.
- **Effort:** L · **Docs:** 01, 08 §9.1, ADR-0006, ADR-0007 · **ADR needed:** Yes (Travel-law ADR).

**R-091 — No detection when merchant checkout price diverges from quote; verified-savings unverifiable (hidden-assumption, L, 2).** The handoff quote is from NEXUS's own data, but authoritative price/coupon acceptance happens on the merchant cart NEXUS never touches (ADR-0006); SDD §9's degradation only covers merchant-API-down, not the common case of a live API returning a quote while the actual cart price/stock/coupon differs. Every "best all-in price" / "verified savings" claim is effectively unenforceable once the user leaves, reproducing the dynamic-pricing pain NEXUS exists to solve, with no user-facing signal. **Rec:** reframe "verified savings" as "verified at quote time" (legal-safe) in copy and API `detail`; add a confirmation-gate disclaimer that the merchant cart is authoritative; where the connector postback includes settled order value, reconcile it against the quoted total and retroactively adjust/suppress the "verified" badge (feeding the R-022 savings-state machine); track a quote-to-postback price-variance metric. **Alt:** post-redirect price re-check where connectors support it; do nothing beyond the staleness badge (largest unaddressed trust risk). **Trade-off:** softening to "quoted savings" dents the viral hook; a reconciliation loop depends on network postback data (R-033 heterogeneity). Docs 02, 03, 07, 04. **ADR: No.**

**R-092 — No return-to-NEXUS UX after the merchant handoff (architectural-weakness, M, 1).** SDD §4.2/07 §4.3 terminate the flow at a redirect to the merchant checkout; nothing specifies how the user returns — no return-URL, in-app-browser overlay, or post-purchase reconciliation screen. A plain web redirect recreates a tab-switch; on mobile it leaves the app — undermining the "one sentence instead of forty tabs" promise, retention/D30, and leaving the savings-receipt (R-022) with no re-entry surface to render on. **Rec:** add `return_url`/`session_resume_token` to the handoff confirm contract; open merchant checkout in an in-app browser/WebView with a persistent back-to-NEXUS affordance on mobile/PWA; the agent thread receives a system message when the postback lands so the conversation self-updates. **Alt:** full navigation-away + email/push for the eventual receipt (reintroduces multi-surface fragmentation). **Trade-off:** in-app browser overlays add engineering + possible merchant-framing TOS friction, but are the difference between "agent" and "link generator." Docs 01, 02, 07. **ADR: No.**

**R-093 — SSE token streaming has no screen-reader announcement strategy (hidden-assumption, M, 1).** NFR-PERF-03 + SSE streaming are specified purely as sighted-latency concerns; no doc specifies how the streamed-token DOM region is exposed to assistive tech. A live node mutating per-token is the textbook WCAG 4.1.3 failure — `aria-live` announces word-salad or (throttled) nothing until final flush. The agent chat IS the product and the safety-critical price/merchant statement is delivered through this channel, so a screen-reader user can't perceive the recommendation the confirmation gate asks them to approve. **Rec:** decouple the visual streaming effect from the accessibility-tree update — render tokens visually as they arrive, update a single `aria-live=polite` region only at sentence/semantic boundaries (or a debounce), and always expose the complete message as a static navigable block once streaming ends; make it an explicit NFR-A11Y-01 acceptance criterion verified by manual screen-reader test (axe can't catch it). **Alt:** no live region + a "read latest message" button (fails 4.1.3 intent); token-by-token assertive (actively worse). **Trade-off:** sentence-boundary debounce adds a small perceived-latency gap for AT users but is the only coherent-speech option. Docs 02, 07, 05. **ADR: No.**

**R-094 — Confirmation gate (the sole safety control) has no a11y/focus-management spec (hidden-assumption, S, 1).** SDD §4.2/07 §4.3/§6.5 call the confirmation gate the core safety promise and the sole control preventing an unapproved (effectively irreversible) handoff, yet no doc specifies accessible-interaction requirements: focus management on open, focus trap, name/role/value of price/merchant/coupon fields, or a keyboard/switch-safe distinction between Approve and dismiss. A screen-reader/switch/voice user could reach "Approve" without the quote content ever being announced — turning the flagship safety mechanism into a rubber stamp for a subset of users. **Rec:** treat the gate as a P0 accessibility component with its own conformance checklist (APG dialog pattern, focus trap, initial focus on the price/merchant summary not the Approve button, full quote as accessible text, clearly-differentiated named controls) + a mandatory manual AT walkthrough as a release gate for any handoff-confirm UI change, referenced from SDD §4.2 and 07 §6.5. **Alt:** rely on the generic axe-core E2E smoke (insufficient — can't detect focus/reading order). **Trade-off:** dedicated component-level a11y work upfront (one dialog) vs. ongoing legal/trust risk of a silently-failing safety control. Docs 02, 07. **ADR: No.**
